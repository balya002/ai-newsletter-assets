AI Industry Snapshot: May was a major month for TV and streaming upfronts, with media companies using the stage to show how AI, data, sports, fandom, and outcomes are shaping the next phase of premium video advertising.
May’s upfronts made one thing clear: AI is now part of the premium video sales story, but each company is using it to answer a different buyer concern — from trust and fandom to automation, creative scale, commerce, contextual relevance, and measurement.
📺 Upfronts 2026: What AI actually means across the major video players
The strongest upfront AI updates were not just about having new tools. They showed where each company wants to compete: Disney around premium environments and trust, Netflix around automated ad operations, WBD around contextual and shoppable formats, YouTube around creator-led commerce, Amazon around purchase signals, and NBCU around live moments.
Disney: Disney’s message was less about chasing AI hype and more about using technology to strengthen a premium, trusted advertising environment. The stronger positioning angle is that Disney can connect brands to high-value fandom, sports, streaming, and cultural moments while still emphasizing quality, safety, and scale.
Insight: Disney’s advantage is not “we have AI too.” It is that AI can sit on top of a differentiated content and data ecosystem. That is a stronger client story than automation alone.

Netflix: Netflix’s upfront update was one of the clearest examples of AI moving into the actual ad operating layer. The company highlighted AI-driven tooling to develop and optimize media plans based on brand objectives, plus AI agents that can help manage, optimize, and purchase ads.
Insight: Netflix is trying to prove it is no longer just a premium content buy. It wants buyers to see it as a more mature ad platform with planning, optimization, and workflow automation built in.

Warner Bros. Discovery: WBD’s update was more product-specific: scene-level contextual targeting powered by KERV.ai, shoppable pause ads, dynamic creative options, and agentic ad solutions. This is AI being applied directly to relevance, commerce, and ad experience design.
Insight: WBD’s pitch shows where CTV may be heading: not just “show my ad before premium content,” but “place my brand near the right moment, make it shoppable, and prove the interaction mattered.”

Insight: YouTube is not only competing for video dollars. It is competing for the entire path from discovery to purchase, which puts pressure on traditional CTV sellers to explain what makes premium video uniquely valuable.

✨ Hidden Feature: Sensitivity Labels in Microsoft 365

Use case: Helping protect internal documents, decks, and spreadsheets before sharing them

Sensitivity Labels can help classify files based on how they should be handled, such as whether they are public, internal, confidential, or restricted. This is especially helpful when working with decks, reports, or documents that may be shared across teams.
Quick example: Before sending a deck or report, check the sensitivity label in the Microsoft 365 toolbar and make sure it matches the content and audience. If the file includes internal strategy, client details, or non-public information, use the appropriate internal or confidential label before sharing.
Try this workflow:

1. Open the file in Word, Excel, or PowerPoint
2. Look for the sensitivity label in the top toolbar
3. Confirm the label matches the content
4. Update the label before sharing if needed

Tip: Labels do not replace good judgment, but they are a helpful guardrail when working with internal or client-related materials.
